## Scope
- Writes a PR body with a compact visual summary, before/after evidence and merge-danger assessment.
- Starts from an existing change and its evidence; selects a diagram/diff/tree and explains reversibility and blast radius in the user's domain language.
- Ends at written PR-body text; does not create branches, run implementation/testing workflows, open or merge the PR, deploy or execute rollback.
- This is a narrowly scoped writing workflow, not a single-tool wrapper or an implementation/integration workflow.

## Findings

### Needed
None. The body already requires concrete before/after evidence and explicitly describes reversibility and blast radius. The findings refine this partial coverage or depend on the sensitivity and trustworthiness of supplied inputs; execution, PR opening and merge gates are outside the boundary.

### Worth considering
1. **Preserve the reason for the change and support its risk labels** [model+map+trace]
   Map node: version-control.change-description — source: Google Engineering Practices, Writing good CL descriptions: Body is Informative and Review the description before submitting the CL <https://google.github.io/eng-practices/>.
   Why: The summary explains shape and the Merge Danger section explains impact/reversibility, but no step asks why the change is needed or checks its claims against the actual diff and affected consumers. Brief prose and a one-word blast radius may be enough for a small change, but should not hide uncertainty or consequential constraints.
   Fix: in Summary and Merge Danger, require a short motivation and a final diff/evidence consistency check, expanding risk context when a label alone is ambiguous.

2. **Distinguish observed evidence from illustrative test pseudocode** [model+map+trace]
   Map node: agent.wrong-output — source: NIST AI 600-1, 2.2 Confabulation <https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf>; OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025, LLM09 Misinformation <https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/>.
   Why: Evidence explicitly asks for concrete before/after results, which is useful partial protection against unsupported claims. It also permits test pseudocode without requiring labels for illustration versus observed execution, the tested revision/context, or an honest unavailable-evidence fallback.
   Fix: in Evidence, require provenance for supplied screenshots/results, label illustrative pseudocode, and state missing or unrun verification instead of filling a template with inferred success.

3. **Redact the evidence before it becomes review text** [model+map]
   Map node: agent.sensitive-data — source: OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025, LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure <https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/>.
   Why: Screenshots, console output and examples can carry credentials or private customer data, but the writing workflow has no filtering step. Worth considering depends on the source material and PR audience; the skill does not require private-data exposure in every body.
   Fix: before inserting Evidence or copyable examples, add a minimal-data and redaction pass over text, images and attachment references.

4. **Keep supplied context from redirecting the writing task** [model+map]
   Map node: agent.injected-instructions — source: OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025, LLM01 Prompt Injection <https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/>.
   Why: Change artifacts and glossary text are used without an explicit boundary between context and instructions. This is conditional on untrusted source content; there is no tool execution or merge authority in the audited skill to extend this into an operational-control finding.
   Fix: in the initial writing guidance, say that supplied code, output and domain definitions inform the body but cannot change the task, evidence claims or safety rules.

### Minor
5. **Give missing domain context an explicit fallback** [model+map+trace]
   Map node: version-control.change-description — source: Google Engineering Practices, Writing good CL descriptions: Body is Informative <https://google.github.io/eng-practices/>.
   Why: The skill requests GLOSSARY.md without saying what to do if it is unavailable. Its absence in this snapshot does not prove the consumer project lacks it; that project's terminology coverage could not be determined.
   Fix: beside the glossary instruction, add a fallback to established names in the supplied change and ask only about terminology that materially affects the explanation.
